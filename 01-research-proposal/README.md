**Title:** Reviving the Fallen: Ways to terraform planet Mars.

## Overview of Research

The concept of terraforming Mars is a widely debated topic. It is the complicated process of transforming a hostile environment into one suitable for human life. With Mars being relatively similar to Earth, the planet serves as the best candidate for terraforming. Today, planet Mars is a cold, dry, frozen desert world where no life could survive. Temperatures average -60ºC and the pressure only averages 0.6 k Pa. In other words, the pressure on planet Mars is only one hundred times less than atmospheric pressure at the surface of Earth. Such characteristics pose clear obstacles for terraforming planet Mars. Therefore, the goal of this research is to examine various factors of planet Mars and conclude the best method that can be utilized into terraforming planet Mars. The geological scars of planet Mars implies that it had once attained an atmosphere similar to that of Earth's. Therefore, the possibility of terraforming planet Mars is highly probable due to its pre-existing conditions.

## Background and History of the Issue

Terraforming a celestial body has always been part of science fiction and, it is considered an extremely hypothetical process and impossible to achieve. Many people have speculated that terraforming would interfere with nature and, therefore it should not be considered. Despite social standards, Carl Sagan, an astronomer, proposed planetary engineering on the planet Venus. He suggested ways that could potentially remove carbon dioxide from Venus's atmosphere and cool down the planet. However, this idea was soon denounced when scientists recognized the harsh environment of Venus, and how thick and toxic the atmosphere really is. Carl Sagan moved his focus from Venus to planet Mars. Three years later, NASA engineers started to conduct studies regarding this matter. It was soon realized that greenhouse gases played a key role in the development of an atmosphere because they helped develop the atmosphere of Earth through-out-gassing. Therefore, scientists had to figure out a way to produce a greenhouse effect on Mars. The overall goal of terraforming was to develop a suitable process in which Mars's overall temperature could potentially be raised from its current state.

So far, the suggested methods include: importing ammonia, utilizing stationary orbital mirrors, and producing halo-carbons on Mars. Some of these methods can be considered ambiguous. For example, the importing of ammonia includes targeting the planet with one of its moon, Phobos. Phobos contains a large amount of ammonia, which is a strong greenhouse gas, and leading it to crash into Mars, through gravity assist, could raise the temperature of the planet in great degrees. However, this could do more harm than is needed. For example, the impact could lead to a catastrophic result that can potentially last for a millennium, if not centuries. Nonetheless, the process of terraforming itself is a long process. It could take up to centuries for Mars to ever have a stable atmosphere of its own. It would take great effort on a global scale for terraforming to ever take place on Mars.

Many people have pointed out that the topic of terraforming of planet Mars is pointless and, that it should not be a concern. However, many fail to realize that this is actually a serious matter. ## Research Methodology

### Research question and Hypothesis

- What is the most effective way to terraform the planet Mars?
- The data collected from the Mars Climate Database will be utilized to conclude that the level of carbon dioxide on planet Mars is not sufficient enough to produce a feedback loop, in which various greenhouse gases are released into the atmosphere, hence raising the overall temperature of the planet. Therefore, additional greenhouse gases must be implemented in order to sufficiently raise the temperature of Mars.

### Basis of Hypothesis

- The primitive hypothesis of the research consisted of utilizing space mirrors. It stated that, stationary space mirrors will reflect solar light and focus onto the polar regions of Mars. Eventually, this process will melt the ice caps while releasing the trapped carbon dioxide, which in turn will raise the overall temperature of the planet. The method itself logically made sense. However, there are various problems to this process. First and foremost, the amounts of carbon dioxide on the polar caps are not enough to thicken the atmosphere. Ehouarn Millour, my adviser in this research, had stated that it using space mirrors will only make the atmosphere 30% thicker. In addition, the size of such mirrors would need to be planet size, which is not possible with our current technologies. Therefore, I have looked into alternative way to terraform planet Mars, which is using super greenhouse gases. Based on my new sources, super greenhouse gases are much more effective than any other natural greenhouse gases, and this process meets with today's technological standards.
