The topic of terraforming planet Mars is an extremely difficult to study. To begin with, most people are unfamiliar with the term "terraforming". It defines a process of converting a celestial body into one that can be harbored with life, similar to planet Earth. The aim of this study was to analyze different methods that could potentially be implemented into initiating the process of terraforming on planet Mars. My primary goal was to determine the characteristics and certain variable that are involved in the formation of an atmosphere. From there I have looked into various methods that supports each of those crucial variables. Out of all the other methods, imposing greenhouse gases proved to be the most promising.

## Methods

### Participants

In order to determine what the best, feasible method for terraforming planet Mars required me to seek professional assistance. Luckily, my mentor, Ehouarn Millour, was kind enough to guide me through the process. Millour is a research engineer at Laboratoire de Meteorologie Dynamique (LMD), and have an in-depth knowledge of the atmosphere of planet Mars. My interview with him was extremely informative, for he had given me different perspective unto the process of terraforming Mars. For instance, he had suggested that even if you are imposing greenhouse gases unto Mars, terraforming might not still take place. This is because it's not just the greenhouse gases that matters, but also the overall condition of the planet; size, pressure, and the existence of a magnetosphere. In addition, Millour insisted that even warming up the planet will not produce a stable atmosphere, because it will only produce a 30% thicker atmosphere, which according to him, is still a thin atmosphere. His suggestions limited the concept of terraforming by a huge degree. However, there are some weaknesses into his suggestion. For instance, due to his busy schedule, he wasn't able to provide me with concrete data regarding the production of a 30% thick atmosphere. Also, he resides in France, which leads to lack of communication, due to different time zones. However, he did provide me with the Mars Climate Database, in which I implemented to illustrate that through careful tending, liquid water on Mars can actually exist.

### Materials

The Mars Climate Database is a set of various forms of data regarding the climatology of planet Mars. It holds immense amount of information about the atmosphere of planet Mars. The interface allows you create your own scenarios with certain variables about the atmosphere. For instance, variables such as landing date, time, altitude, interest, coordinates, and factors such as temperature, wind and water column all can be implemented.

### Procedure

From the Mars Climate Database, I have gathered various atmospheric images that illustrated the level of certain chemical compounds over the entire Martian surface. There are two chemical compounds that are necessary for greenhouse production. One being the most universal compound of all time, water, and the other being carbon dioxide. These compounds are one of many greenhouse gases that can prove to be beneficial in the process of terraforming. Therefore, from the Mars Climate Database, I have used four crucial variables for the atmospheric scenario. These variables are as follows:

- ***Temperature (K)***
- ***Surface CO2 Ice Layer (kg/m2)***
- ***Surface H2O Ice Layer (kg/m2, 0.5: perennial)***
- ***CO2 Column (kg/m2)***

The Mars Climate Database allows you to implement these variables in order to generate color coded images with the variables attached. I have decided to gather all images ranging from 0.0 hour to 24.0 hour, totaling 240 images, off all four given variables. Then I gather these images and compress them into one video file under one frame per each image. This will provide a sort of time-lapse of the evolution of certain chemical compounds on Mars over one day. Better yet, I have implemented this procedure by collecting four more Martian days derived from different time of the year. This goes further by illustrating an evolution of Mars's atmospheric composition throughout the year. From there I took various observational notes regarding the level of each of the variables throughout the days as well as the years.

## Result

The data collected from the Mars Climate Database was analyzed through a series of observations. I categorized these observations between different time of the year, and distinguished between the given variables. However, due to clear pattern in each of the dates, summary of the observations are listed below.

The data reveals that there is a direct correlation between the level of temperature and the level of H2O ice layer. For instance, temperature at 250-300 Kelvin results in no H2O ice layer at all. However, if the temperature is toned down, around 200 Kelvin, an H2O ice layer begins accumulating the surface. This is only apparent at regions ranging from -60 to 60 degrees. In addition, as expected, temperature increases dramatically during midday, this is also when the level of H2O ice layer increases. On the other hand, the level of the CO2 Column relatively stayed the same with slight variations throughout the hours, but increased in the later season. For instance, if it was summer in the North or the South, an observer could easily interpret that there is indeed a strong influxes of carbon dioxide throughout the day. One of the key pieces of information that was learned through these observations was that the water ice layer is existent under the direct influence of relative amount of warmth. This is a clear indication that water ice layer can be produced if the tending of Mars was balanced; not too hot and not too cold.

### Interview with Christopher McKay

Tahalil Morsilin

Christopher McKay

Planetary System Branch  
NASA Ames Astrobiologist  
Interviewed March 23, 2015

Prior to the interview, I had emailed Christopher McKay via email requesting whether he would be willing to take a look at my synthesis paper. After a couple days, McKay replied and was interested in what I was working on. Later, I called him through my cell phone directly, and we were able to set up an interview. The interview had taken place on March 23, 2015, at 4:00 PM EST. I had contacted him via Google Plus hangout. Unfortunately, since I had spoken with him through my desktop, I was not able to record the actual conversation. However, knowing the circumstances, I was able to take notes regarding the interview.

My first question stressed the importance of various factors upon the existence of life elsewhere in the universe. Despite many other chemical compounds that is required for life to actually evolve, I inquired Dr. Mckay on what is the most crucial component factor that is required to life to actually exist? As expected, McKay stressed the importance of water being a crucial factor in the development of life. He points out that water is almost everywhere on Earth and that is where we find the most of life. Even in the deepest point in our ocean, life is present. The point he was trying to make is that, whenever you have a liquid environment, life is bound for existence. However, that is not the case with outer space. McKay indicates that water is a crucial constraints in space. He provides example on Mars, such that liquid water does not exist at all on Mars. He concludes that if a planet has LIQUID water, life will be able to thrive and he and other scientist will be very optimistic about that particular planet.

From there I questioned him whether or not life exist on Mars, since life is an important factor of terraforming. His answer was particularly interesting because he states that some organism can stay in a dormant states in which they are technically alive, but not performing metabolism. This actually allows the organism to stay fresh for a long period of time. He indicates that this is how Earth might have gotten its "life package". And that could be the same case for Mars. If the planet does contain these dormant organisms, and if the conditions of Mars is tweaked at the right setting, these dormant organism might, according to Dr. McKay, wake up and began to develop and evolve.

The idea or the notion of producing another Earth like planet in our solar system is a profound concept. I requested Dr. McKay to talk a little bit regarding the possibility of bringing Mars back to life. His response was actually quite profounds and added a brand new perspective into reviving Mars. For instance, McKay insisted on that not only human life, but all life should be spread to Mars in order to revive its biospheres.

My next question to Dr. McKay was actually based on one of its paper. I asked him that if Mars already contains life, should we as human beings continue to terraform the planet? Basically, what are the ethical implications of terraforming planet Mars? Dr. McKay stated that if life does exist on Mars, than we should leave it alone. If not, then the whole planet would end up being contaminated.The reason behind this is because if life is present on Mars, it might be in the process of developing. Sending humans and probes to it will disrupt that process, and that will prove to be morally wrong. Dr. McKay goes onto indicate that humans can not alter nature for the sake of its will because it needs to consider the ethical implications of it.
