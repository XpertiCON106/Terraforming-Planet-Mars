## Reviving the Fallen: Terraforming Planet Mars

Planet Earth is nothing but a microbe, lurking within the vastness of the Universe. Just recently, asteroid 2004 BL86 swept through the orbit of planet Earth by three lunar distances. It may have seemed far away, but in astronomical perspective, it was very close. The asteroid itself had its own natural satellite, which though small, could wipe out the very life that is harbored here on Earth. The possibility of an object 70 meter across devastating a planet of 7,918 miles across is unfathomable. If the course of asteroid BL86 had taken an alternative route, perhaps one directed toward Earth itself, humanity would have perished. The world is very fragile and it is no surprise that scientists are looking for new worlds. However, there are planets in the solar system that are so inhospitable that no Earthly life can ever take hold. Terraforming is the process which can convert these vacant worlds into one that can be harbored with life. It is the very process that humanity must master in order to continue its survival as a race. Planet Mars serves to be the best candidate for terraforming because its existing conditions are much more favorable than that of the other planets in our solar system. Despite the fact that the methods of terraforming mars are currently far beyond human capability and are impractical, importing greenhouse gases is the most promising method for the future.

The topic of terraforming can be considered part science fiction, because it involves an extremely hypothetical process and perhaps one that is impossible to achieve. It all began with Carl Sagan, a prominent astronomer, questioning planetary engineering on the planet Venus. His ideas proposed removal of carbon dioxide from Venus, which would convert the planet into a habitable place. Later, scientific research concluded that the harsh environment of Venus would not allow terraforming to take hold. Sagan later shifted his studies toward Mars, and realized that terraforming might be possible on the red planet. NASA engineers soon started to conduct studies on terraforming, aiming to produce a greenhouse effect on Mars. The overall goal of terraforming was to develop a suitable process in which Mars's overall temperature could potentially be raised from its current state. In other words, they hoped to mimic the process of how the atmosphere had developed here on planet Earth.

In order to understand terraforming in its core, it is crucial to first understand how Earth had developed its own atmosphere, which was the result of out-gassing of various greenhouse gases and the arrival of life. Each factor of development has its own characteristics which aided to the formation of a stable atmosphere, and in each of these factors, the production of greenhouse gases is inevitable. Early Earth's atmosphere was mainly composed of carbon dioxide. However, 2.3 million years ago, the level of oxygen had begun to accumulate in the atmosphere in three distinct stages. For instance, Perry Samson, professor of atmospheric sciences at University of Michigan, states that the first level of oxygen accumulation is "through [the] photolysis of water vapor and carbon dioxide by ultraviolet energy and, [or] possibly lightning" (Samson). Photolysis is a term for a chemical reaction in which H₂O is broken into hydrogen (H₂) and oxygen (O), and the same is concurrent with CO₂ where it is broken into carbon monoxide (CO) and oxygen, which is a clear indication of oxygen accumulating in the Earth's atmosphere. However, since oxygen is a diatomic element, O by itself is not stable, but rather O₂ is much more stable. The chemical reaction between O and OH produced O₂ and hydrogen (Samson). The availability of oxygen allowed for the process of photosynthesis to actually occur within organisms such as blue-green algae, and as a result, reduced the level of CO₂ while increasing O₂. The very common formula of photosynthesis 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ reveals how the level of accumulation of oxygen had increased due to organism undergoing such process. In fact, this very process gave rise to complex organism that continued to aid in the accumulation of oxygen in the atmosphere ("Origins of Life"). However it is crucial to understand how these organisms appeared to begin with.

Life is the very force that drives the diversity of this planet. The question of how organism had appeared out of mere rocks has long been speculated through human interpretations as well as scientific explanation. However, researchers at University of Leeds found that "phosphorus chemical…could be [a] precursor to the batteries that now power all life on Earth" ("Origins of Life"). Life depends upon a molecule known as Adenosine tri-phosphate (ATP) in order serve as the fuel for metabolism and transfer energy. Scientist has speculated that "meteorite phosphorus may have been a fuel that provided the energy and phosphorus necessary for the onset of life" (Chachere). In fact, the meteorite phosphorus was tested in an experiment when it was placed in an acid from a geothermal area in Iceland. The experiment detected a compound named pyrophosphite, which is actually similar to pyrophosphate, and it too can be used for energy transfer ("Origins of life"). What is surprising is that if the meteorite phosphorus is implemented in organic compounds, the phosphorus bio-molecules generated are identical to those present in today's life (Chachere). The arrival of life and oxygen accumulation clearly contributed to the development of Earth's atmosphere. Regardless of life being characterized as a fundamental requirement for the development of the atmosphere, and in addition to oxygen accumulation, there is another abiotic force that ultimately shaped the fate of Earth's atmosphere.

The production of greenhouse gases through outgassing further developed the atmosphere of Earth by raising the temperature and further thickening it. Oxygen did not suddenly began to increase in the atmosphere due to photosynthesis, rather for a long period of time, submarine volcanoes striped away the oxygen by producing reducing mixture of gases, however, as these submarine volcanoes rose and became terrestrial volcanoes, atmospheric oxygen were released and accumulated the atmosphere ("Volcanoes Key to Earth's). According to United States Geological Survey (2010), more than 70% of the output of volcanoes is water, which is a clear indication of outgassing leading to life, and ultimately lead to a stable atmosphere; water is a greenhouse compound, and will raise the overall temperature of the planet. In theory, Earth's atmosphere was the result of various greenhouse gases such as CO₂ and H₂O reacting with each other, and life itself being part of the endless cycle of photosynthesis, and the production of outgassing, which released even more greenhouse gases. These classes of chemical compounds gave rise to the atmosphere of planet Earth. This process must take place again on planet Mars in order for terraforming to actually occur.

Some may question, why planet Mars? Why choose this red planet out of seven other planets in our solar system? Mars is the only planet that holds existing conditions for terraforming to take place. Terraforming Venus is far beyond current human capabilities because it requires a reversed process of terraforming, or cooling down the planet. Warming a planet is much easier than it is to cool a planet. Terraforming of all the gas giants such a Jupiter and Saturn is impossible due to the lack of solid surfaces, and Mercury is just way too hot and inhospitable for life to ever take hold. Water and greenhouse gases are an important factor into terraforming, and luckily Mars already contains such chemical compounds.

New research published in the prestigious international scientific journal ICARUS suggests that liquid water is present on Mars that is old as 200,000 years old. The evidence for liquid water on Mars is indicated by the comparison of the "landforms on Mars with known debris flows on Svalbard with the aid of aerial photography and [various] field studies" (Eliasson). Andreas Johnson, professor in the department of Earth Sciences at the University of Gothenburg, states that the "water has come from melting snow packs, when the conditions were favorable for snow formation" (Eliasson). The evidence of water for this particular study was found within an impact crater, which could be an indication of water existing elsewhere in the universe, and being transported through meteorites. Whatever the case, the existence of water on Mars is supported by additional evidence. The current Mars mission, Curiosity, has found various traces of water on the surface of Mars. The rover scooped up four portions of the Martian soil and discovered 2% water, and the soil that was later heated revealed various elemental compounds that can only exist under the presence of water (Lieberman). In addition to water, Mars already contains one of the strongest greenhouse gases of all time.

Recently, observations by the Sample Analysis at Mars (SAM) instruments equipped with the Curiosity rover detected a large concentration of methane outside the gale craters which suggested that either chemical reaction between microbes, water and Olivine rocks have released methane, or it is the byproduct of volcanic activity (Reyes). The existence of methane can prove to be beneficial because it can be implemented to raise the temperature of planet Mars to 15 degree Celsius, and be further extended with extensive human efforts.

According to one of the leading scientist in Terraforming, Christopher McKay, a stable atmosphere will "require large amounts of CO₂ and H₂O and [especially] N₂… [which] is essential for a breathable atmosphere and…is needed by life as an essential macronutrient" (McKay 249). Also, melting the carbon dioxide at the polar regions of Mars will "cause the complete evaporation of [the] ice through a positive feedback mechanism" (McKay 253). Dr. McKay goes onto state that, the production of CO₂ in Mars might take up to 100 years, and a "breathable oxygen rich atmosphere will take more than 100,000 years." Even though the time frame surpasses the existence of human civilization by 88,000 years, super-greenhouse gases will be able to increase the rate of the warming of planet Mars. For example, gases such as chlorofluorocarbons sulfur hexafluoride and hexafluoroethane all serve as candidates for considerable super-greenhouse gases (Gerstell et al). Scientists at the division of geological and planetary sciences at California Institute of Technology have tested their theories with five of these super greenhouse gases and found SF₅CF₃, or Trifluoromethyl Sulfur Pentafluoride to be extremely beneficial toward terraforming (Gerstell et al). In addition to SF₅CF₃, Perfluorocarbons is another super greenhouse gas that could be used for warming Mars. The compound holds long durability and does not impose any threat to living organism (Phillips). Considering the existing condition of Mars, and the availability of super greenhouse gases, terraforming of planet Mars almost seems possible. However, these resources by themselves cannot complete the job, for the process of terraforming is solely dependent on the methods of implementation.

The methods that have been proposed for terraforming Mars are not possible with current technologies. Although it will take a long time, importing greenhouse gases is the best method for terraforming Mars. However, it is important to examine other methods and analyze why they would not be feasible within current technological standards.

One of the methods for terraforming is building space originated mirrors to direct sunlight towards the polar caps in order to melt the frozen carbon dioxide. For this to work, the mirrors themselves need to be 125 km in order to reflect "enough sunlight to raise [an]…area's [temperature] by 5 Kelvin" (Zubrin & McKay 1993). To put this into perspective, the largest rocket ever constructed by NASA, the mighty Saturn V stands tall at only 353 feet. Building a space satellite that is 125 km in diameter, is impossible with current technological standards. Some may believe that building mirror out of lighter resources might be feasible. However, if the mirrors is "made of solar sail type aluminized Mylar material with a density of 4 tons/km² (3628.74 kg/km²), such a sail would have mass of 200,000 tons (Zubrin & McKay 1993). Again, two hundred thousand tons is approximately 400,000,019 pounds. Today, it costs \$10,000 to launch a pound of payload into Earth orbit. To put it into perspective, it would cost around \$4,409,250,000,000 just to launch this space based mirror satellite. Even if the space mirror is feasible and sent to Mars, another problem would arise as well. For example, in order for the satellite to warm a certain region over a constant period of time, it would need to follow a geostationary orbit with zero inclination and eccentricity (Zubrin & McKay 1993). Overtime, the gravitational attraction between Mars, its moons, and the Sun would affect the orbit of the stationed mirrors. Therefore, periodic maintenance would be required, which would prove to be extremely costly (Kelso).

Another method for terraforming planet Mar is actually proposed by a prominent physicist, Michio Kaku. He argues for directing an asteroid towards Mars in order to quickly raise the overall temperature of planet. He envisions controlling the orbit of meteorite and enter it into Mars while slowly release the stored water vapor inside the meteorite (Kaku). Humans do not have the technology to divert or to alter the course of another celestial body. Capturing an asteroid would require humans to reach the asteroid belt, which is much farther than Mars. In addition, it would require a rocket to actually propel the asteroid using its own material. If the asteroid itself is filled with ammonia, the rocket can use it power nuclear thermal rocket engines through the process of either fusion or fission (Zubrin & McKay 1993).

It seems as if the proposed methods for terraforming planet Mars are directly derived from science fiction. However, many aspects of science today were once considered science fiction. For examples, lasers only belonged in the highest realm of fantasy. Now lasers are being utilized in all applications of science ranging from military use to merely playing with a house cat. Therefore, it is not sensible to declare such methods as being science fiction due to its complex level of feasibility. In fact, the only reason such methods are not feasible because of the lack of resources that is required to implement it. Regardless of the lack of resources, transporting greenhouse gases, although time costly, to planet Mars in order to release and eventually raise the temperature of the planet is the best method for terraforming. Implementing coal derived from Earth on the "Martian surface to produce CO₂ through combustion...would increase the atmospheric density to that similar to Earth" (Longmen et al). The amount of coal that is needed to actually accumulate the carbon dioxide is3.5 × 10¹⁹ kg, and the energy required to actually transport the coal is 1.97 × 10²⁷ Joules (Longmen et al). Likewise, the problem with energy and resources rises, because where will the energy and resources come from? Building 100 nuclear powered factories that emit greenhouse gases over a long period of time could be a possibility. Overtime, Mars's temperature could be raised to six to eight degrees (Phillips). At this rate, in order to increase the average Martian temperature to the melting point of water, about −66° C, would take about eight centuries. It is a long time frame, but it is possible. Building factories to produce greenhouse gases is within the current technological standards, because humans have already been doing that since the industrialization age. Earth is constantly being filled with various greenhouse gases that continue to melt the glaciers, causing the depletion of the ozone layer, and ultimately raising Earth's overall temperature. Therefore, implementing this methodology on Mars to produce greenhouse gases is feasible, and within current technological standards.

The question of terraforming Mars really comes down to one point; what are the methods that are needed and how should it be implemented in order to actually get the job done? And what resources are available on Earth that will allow terraforming to take place? Methods such as orbital mirror deflecting sunlight onto the Polar Regions, and directing an asteroid toward Mars to raise the temperature is far beyond human capabilities. However, these methods cannot be declared as impossible, for they are indeed possible, but not within the current technological standards. In addition, implementing these methods would require a new form fuel. The ordinary combustion fuel will not amount to terraforming planet Mars. Rather, new forms of fuel system such as anti-matter (opposite of matter) will need to be introduced. These are feats that human civilizations have yet to achieve. Building factories on Mars to produce greenhouse gases, however, is within current technological standards. Although it will take a long amount of time, Mars's temperature will gradually rise due to human activities and terraforming of planet Mars will be inevitable. Terraforming Mars will not be a one day challenge; rather it will be a slow and steady pace in which greenhouse gases will be released into the Martian atmosphere. In addition, the topic of terraforming is not a call for panic; rather it is a call for the possibility of human exploration beyond planet Earth. Through the study of terraforming, humans will be able to understand many fundamental principles of applied science. Not only will it increase the probability of human survival, terraforming will increase human knowledge in its entirety.
